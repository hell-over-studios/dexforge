# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Fan-made Pokémon players who are building a team for one specific main-series game
(Red/Blue, Gold/Silver, Emerald today; more games later). They arrive from a browser,
often on a phone, while playing or planning a run, and want to know whether a team
works in _that_ game — not in a generic national dex. They speak Spanish (default) or
English. No account, no install, no companion app.

## Product Purpose

Dexforge is a fan-made team builder where the player picks a main-series Pokémon game
and builds a team of up to six Pokémon available in that game. It exists to make
game-accurate team decisions visible: per-game stats, types, abilities and artwork;
defensive weaknesses and resistances; team averages and roles; moves and offensive
coverage; where to find each Pokémon; and how the team fares against gym leaders and
the Elite Four. Success means a player can answer "does this team work in my game?"
without opening five wikis or doing type-chart arithmetic by hand.

## Positioning

Game accuracy by construction: every screen is computed from that game's own roster,
type chart and rules (Gen-I quirks included), delivered as static data generated
offline from PokeAPI. A generic national-dex team builder cannot truthfully copy the
per-game chart, the version-group roster or the availability constraints.

## Operating Context

- Static Astro site deployed to GitHub Pages; no runtime calls to PokeAPI.
- Data is generated by `/scripts` into `/public/data` and committed; curated overrides
  live in `/data/curated`.
- Teams are held in the browser (localStorage) and shared by URL; no accounts.
- Spanish is the default UI language, English is a first-class alternative.
- The project is open source (MIT) and maintained by an autonomous agent under the
  owner's review at MINOR/MAJOR release boundaries.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Current: game selector; searchable per-game Pokédex; detail drawer with official
  artwork, types, abilities and base stats; team of six; share by URL; game-accurate
  defensive table; team average radar and role heuristic; defensive notes.
- Planned for v0.4.0: moves data per game, abilities view, move picker + share payload
  v2, offensive coverage matrix.
- Planned later: encounter locations, gym leader / Elite Four matchups, Showdown
  import/export, multiple saved teams, more games.
- Constraints: official artwork only for species visuals (owner directive 2026-11-07);
  no ripped 3D models; no paid services; no runtime PokeAPI; mobile-first; keyboard
  accessible; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; Spanish default + English.
- Explicitly undecided: additional games and their version-group scope; 3D viewer;
  accounts or cloud sync.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Dexforge**. Fan-made, not affiliated with Nintendo, Game Freak or The Pokémon
  Company; Pokémon names and images belong to their owners.
- Visual identity: **pixel art + cozy** (owner directive 2026-11-06, ADR-008).
- Owner-approved revision 2026-11-16, "Pixel-cozy v2 — Pergamino": parchment panels over
  a deep forest ground, larger pixel display type, solid legible type chips, official
  artwork staged as a field-journal diorama. This supersedes the warm-bark/dark-panel
  rendition without changing the pixel-cozy mandate.
- Spanish is the default voice; English is not an afterthought.

## Product Principles

1. **The game is the source of truth.** Every chart, roster and rule is scoped to the
   selected game before it is simplified for display.
2. **Decisions beat data dumps.** A panel exists to answer a team-building question,
   not to show that the data exists.
3. **Cozy is a welcome, not a skin.** The interface should feel like a field journal
   opened on a log, warm and legible, even when the news is "four of your six are weak
   to Ice".
4. **No accounts, no waiting.** Static data, instant interactions, shareable URLs.
5. **Spanish first, English equal.** Copy is authored in both languages, not machine
   pasted at the end.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Keyboard-first combobox/listbox navigation with visible focus rings.
- AA contrast for text and data chips; color is never the only carrier of meaning
  (multipliers and labels are written out).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` clamps entrance and idle animation.
- Mobile-first layouts verified at 360px; touch targets sized for thumbs.
